The Word of Flawed

“A book that causes confusion, quarrels, fights, division and even wars cannot be of divine origin, unless God is stupid and sadistic.”— Paulo Bittencourt

How many times have we heard Christians call that heap of copies of copies of more copies of rags of fragments of parchments from the Iron Age, with myths also from the Bronze Age, the Word of God! How many times have we heard Christians assert that Christianity is the true religion!

Isn’t it natural to expect that the intelligence of a being who creates a universe with trillions of galaxies is infinitely greater than ours and, therefore, if he decided to reveal himself through texts, that his book would be written with such clarity and precision that it would be impossible not to understand it or to misinterpret it? If a religion were invented based on a book with such clear and precise instructions and if for the Creator of the Universe the greatest thing is love, would it be possible for even just one of its practitioners to commit an act of violence? How could a divine book motivate its readers to persecute and kill each other and those who, for whatever reasons, reject it?

Because of the Bible many millions of people, Christians and non-Christians, were killed. Millions! Christianity is based on a book supposedly of divine origin, yet it’s the most divided religion. Even though they no longer persecute and kill each other over the “Word of God” (since secular laws prohibit them from doing so), Christians still today fight over it. There are thousands of Christian denominations, and each of them claims that all the others are wrong, going so far as to teach that members of the wrong denominations are, after death, tortured in a lake of fire and brimstone.

There are hundreds of doctrinal disagreements within Christianity, but they can be organized into a manageable set of major categories. Not every denomination disagrees on every point, and some disagreements are considered essential while others are relatively minor. Below is a broad doctrinal map of the major issues on which Christian traditions differ (many of which were reasons why Christians spent centuries persecuting and killing each other).

100. What Counts as “Christian Doctrine”?

Some Christians define Christianity primarily through Nicene orthodoxy: Trinity + Incarnation + Resurrection + Salvation through Christ. Others require:

  • Apostolic succession
  • Sacraments
  • Catholic/Orthodox communion
  • Protestant confessions
  • Biblical inerrancy
  • Believer’s baptism
  • Particular doctrines of justification

There is disagreement not only within Christian doctrine, but over which doctrines are necessary to qualify as Christian in the first place.

The Disagreements Can Ultimately Be Grouped Into About 12 Giant Questions

In seeking to understand why Christianity split into Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant, Anglican, Lutheran, Reformed, Baptist, Methodist, Pentecostal, Anabaptist, etc., the enormous list above can be compressed into these:

  1. What is the authority? (Scripture/Scripture + Tradition/Church)
  2. Who is God? (Trinity/non-Trinitarian alternatives)
  3. Who is Jesus? (Different Christologies)
  4. What happened at The Fall? (Original sin/Ancestral sin/Other views)
  5. How free is Humanity? (Calvinism ↔ Arminianism ↔ Catholic/Orthodox synergism)
  6. How are we saved? (Faith alone/Faith + Works/Sacramental participation/Theosis)
  7. Can salvation be lost? (Perseverance/Conditional security)
  8. What is the Church? (Catholic/Orthodox/Protestant/Invisible church models)
  9. What are baptism and Eucharist? (Sacramental realities ↔ Symbolic ordinances)
  10. Who has authority? (Pope/Bishops/Councils/Pastors/Congregation/Individual)
  11. What happens at The End? (Different resurrection, millennium, rapture, judgment and Hell models)
  12. How should Christians live? (Different positions on law, Sabbath, sex, marriage, politics, war, etc.)
